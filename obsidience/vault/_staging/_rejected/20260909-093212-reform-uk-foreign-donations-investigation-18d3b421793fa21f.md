The Metropolitan Police Special Enquiry Team is conducting a criminal investigation into Reform UK over allegations it broke foreign funding laws under the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000.

## Origin

The probe followed a Channel 4 News undercover broadcast in which two senior Reform aides discussed circumventing electoral law to secure a £500,000 foreign donation:

- **Dan Jukes** (chief of staff to Nigel Farage) suggested the donation could be made in the UK-based son's name of an American donor, despite the money coming from the father. Electoral law requires a donor to be a UK registered voter or a UK-registered company.
- **James Orr** (head of policy) suggested the American father could pay for opinion polling to support Reform's campaigns.

## Fallout

- Dan Jukes stepped back from politics to "clear my name" and denied any wrongdoing.
- James Orr said he would cooperate with Reform's internal investigation.
- Both lost their positions after the broadcast overshadowed the party's conference in Birmingham.

## Broader probe

The criminal investigation is folded into an existing inquiry into other donations, including direct donations from Fiona Cottrell (mother of Farage ally George Cottrell) and a £1m payment to Reform's deputy leader Richard Tice's company from Fiona Cottrell in June 2024.

## Positions

- **Met Police:** "All allegations under investigation relate to potential offences under the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000."
- **Reform UK:** "denies any wrongdoing and will fully cooperate with the investigation."
