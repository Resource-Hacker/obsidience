BBC News report (2026-09-09): Christy Spivey has told the BBC she is still waiting for answers from officials more than a month after her 29-year-old daughter, Tasia Fortune, was found hanging from a tree in Jackson, Mississippi, on 3 August.

Police confirmed on 9 September that they were investigating Fortune’s death as a potential homicide, clarifying they had previously described it as a death investigation pending autopsy results. Chief RaShall Brackney said the department had been investigating “from day one as a homicide” and was conducting the inquiry “at the highest appropriate level while we await the medical examiner’s findings.” The medical examiner’s office declined to comment. The official cause and manner of death have not yet been confirmed.

Spivey said Fortune had travelled from Mississippi to Kentucky for a family reunion on 26 July — the last time she was seen alive — and had spoken about “maybe even coming home.” At the funeral, Spivey said she could “barely look” at her daughter’s body.

Fortune’s death has prompted wider concern in the majority-black city, where residents have launched two petitions (over 50,000 signatures for an independent investigation into Mississippi chief medical examiner Dr Staci Turner, and one requesting an FBI investigation into Fortune’s death). Congressman Kenneth Stokes told the BBC no officers had been seen near the home where Fortune was thought to have lived, and that the case “should rise to the top of the food chain,” but said he had yet to hear back from federal prosecutors or the FBI. Congresswoman Jasmine Crockett has requested a federal investigation, citing Mississippi’s “appalling historical reputation of violent terrorism against black Americans.”

Fortune’s death was the third incident in Mississippi in less than a year where police found someone dead and hanged from a tree. The other two (one black male, one white, discovered on the same day in September 2025) were ruled a suicide, though the mother of one deceased — Trey Reed — contests those findings.

The BBC has contacted Mississippi US Attorney’s Office, the local FBI office, and the US Department of Justice for comment.
