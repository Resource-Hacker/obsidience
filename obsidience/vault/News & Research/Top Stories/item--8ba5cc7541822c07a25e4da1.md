## The 9/11 intelligence failure

No one can question that 9/11 was a "colossal intelligence failure" — a failure of imagination in the US intelligence community. The 9/11 Commission Report blamed "failures of imagination, policy, capabilities and management": the CIA and FBI failed to properly assess intelligence and share information with each other.

## Improvements since 9/11

- Sharing between Western intelligence agencies is "infinitely better" than in 2001.
- The UK's MI5 and Police Counter Terrorism now share in a high-security Counter Terrorism Operations Centre, often sitting "literally right next to" each other.
- The US established a National Counter Terrorism Center and a Director of National Intelligence to coordinate across its 18 intelligence agencies.
- MI6 reorganised its assessment of raw field intelligence (CX), separating agent runners from the reports team who "check and challenge" submissions.

## Failures that persisted

Despite these changes, major failures continued:

- **Iraq 2003:** MI6 allowed intelligence to be politicised under pressure to show evidence that Iraq possessed Weapons of Mass Destruction. The Butler Review concluded the UK's intelligence on Iraq's alleged WMD programme "was seriously flawed and heavily reliant on unverified sources".

- **7/7 London bombings (2005):** MI5 was aware of Mohammed Sidique Khan but failed to prevent the attacks that killed 52.

- **Manchester Arena bombing (2017):** Another lapse in security assessment.

## The changing threat landscape

Lord Jonathan Evans, former MI5 director (2007–2013), noted the threat picture shifted. Beyond al-Qaeda, "state threats… have become increasingly serious and are at least as severe a national security concern as non-state terrorism" — from Russia, Iran, proxies and China.

Sir Alex Younger, former MI6 chief, said: "We missed the rise of China as a military power and as a strategic challenge". Sam Olsen of Sibylline added that the West "understands a great deal about China" but "sees the individual pieces without always understanding what they add up to" — describing the problem as "less a failure of intelligence collection than a failure of strategic interpretation".

## Moral questions

The post-9/11 period saw widespread fear of a second wave, leading to human rights abuses including rendition, "extraordinary rendition", and the establishment of Guantanamo Bay. In 2004, MI6 was complicit in the abduction of Abdelhakim Belhaj and his wife from Thailand to Libya, where they were tortured. A 2018 apology was delivered to Parliament.

## The wider picture

The article notes that failures of intelligence are not exclusive to the West — citing Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine and Israel's 7 October 2023 attack on Hamas as comparable examples.

"It is in the big, strategic, national decisions that questions are still being asked," the article concludes: "Was it right for Britain to join President Bush's invasion of Iraq? Was it right to join the US-led Operation Enduring Freedom in Afghanistan and then abandon the country to the Taliban in a dash for the exit two decades later?".
