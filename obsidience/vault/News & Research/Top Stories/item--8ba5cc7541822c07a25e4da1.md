## Source
*BBC News — 10 September 2026* by Frank Gardner

Twenty-five years after the 9/11 attacks defined a generation of foreign, security and intelligence policy, lessons have been partially learnt but new strategic gaps remain. The 2001 attacks resulted from a "colossal intelligence failure" — the CIA and FBI possessed information that was not shared in time.

## What improved
Since 2001, inter-agency intelligence sharing has improved dramatically. The UK established fusion centres and the high-security Counter Terrorism Operations Centre in London, where MI5 officers sit alongside police and FBI/GCHQ specialists. The US created a National Counter Terrorism Center and a Director of National Intelligence to coordinate across 18 agencies. The CIA and MI6 relationship is now "the closest one in the world."

## Persistent failures
Despite progress, major failures persist: MI5 failed to prevent the 2005 7/7 London bombings and the 2017 Manchester Arena bombing. The 2003 Iraq invasion proceeded on "deeply flawed intelligence" — MI6 provided politicised evidence of Iraq's non-existent WMDs, resulting in the organisation's worst reputational setback since the Cold War. Human rights stains remain: the US maintains detention without trial at Guantanamo Bay (and deported over 200 men to El Salvador's Terrorism Confinement Centre in 2025), while MI6 was complicit in the 2004 abduction and torture of Libyan Islamist Abdelhakim Belhaj.

## What was missed
The biggest strategic failure was overlooking the rise of Russia and China. After 20 years focusing on counter-terrorism, Western intelligence failed to see Russia's rearmament and China's rise as a military and strategic challenge. China remains a "hard target" for collection due to advances in biometric surveillance, and the core problem has shifted to "a failure of strategic interpretation" — assembling pieces without understanding the bigger picture.

## The world has changed
AI and quantum computing are accelerating the pace of change. New failures continue to emerge: Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine and Israel's 2023 Hamas-led "own 9/11" from Gaza. The enduring question remains: having intelligence is not the same as applying it for strategic gain.
