# Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch denies housing benefit cuts plan would make people homeless

## Reporting details
- **Published:** 10 September 2026, 11:43 UTC
- **Source:** BBC News
- **Reporting URL:** https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cj9xznl0yvzo
- **Activating Source:** source://7e8b2c54-bf61-427c-8a1d-001d384b2d7f

## Summary
Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has denied that her party's plans to cut housing benefit would make people homeless, though she suggested some would have to "move somewhere cheaper."

At Prime Minister's Questions on Wednesday, Badenoch challenged Labour leader Andy Burnham to commit to spending 3% of GDP on defence by 2030, arguing the cost could be covered by welfare cuts. She proposed cutting £4bn from the housing benefit bill, in part by allowing tenancies in expensive areas to come to an end.

## Badenoch's position
On the Today programme, Badenoch said: > "What we're doing is making sure that people are protected, but capping the amount that people get on benefits because we need to look after our national security. Without national security, there aren't going to be any benefits. We're all going to be in danger, far more people will be homeless, and the government's first duty is defence of the realm."

She said people living in expensive areas would need to "move somewhere cheaper," arguing taxpayers should not "pay for you to live wherever you want to live."

## Labour's response
Andy Burnham argued at PMQs that "national security can't come at the expense of social security," warning the policy would create "levels of homelessness in this country... that we wouldn't have seen before, hundreds of thousands of children in temporary accommodation."

## Context
The Conservatives' plan forms part of a proposed £23bn cut to welfare to partly fund an increase in defence spending. Badenoch said the cuts would be needed "now" rather than later.
