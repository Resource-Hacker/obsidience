**Source:** BBC News  — Theo Leggett  — 10 September 2026

> The price of oil has jumped to $105 a barrel amid signs the conflict in the Middle East will not be resolved quickly, fuelling fears that inflation could accelerate.

## Key developments

- **Oil prices:** Brent crude rose above $100 a barrel on 10 September 2026, climbing to $105 amid intensifying conflict in the Gulf.
- **Gas prices:** UK wholesale natural gas rose above 200p a therm for the first time since the end of 2022.
- **Supply disruptions:** The Strait of Hormuz is effectively closed, preventing Gulf oil and gas supplies from reaching global markets. Iran-aligned Houthi forces were reported to have seized Yemen's port of Mocha, a key Red Sea port, elevating fears of further shipping disruptions.
- **Bond markets:** UK 10-year bond yields reached their highest level in decades; 20- and 30-year yields hit levels not seen since 1998.

## Implications

Higher yields imply a higher cost of borrowing for the government amid fiscal pressure. UK consumers are protected in the short term by Ofgem's price cap, but households may face steeper bills if prices remain elevated for an extended period.

President Trump, speaking at a Republican Party convention, stated he did not expect the fighting to end until after the US mid-term elections in November.
