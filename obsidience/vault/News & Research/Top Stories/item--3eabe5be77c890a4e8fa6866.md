**9 September 2026** — Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood has told police chiefs that lessons must be learned from how they handled last weekend's anti-migrant protests in Dover and Portsmouth, saying she expects officers to use all powers necessary.

## What happened

In a meeting on Thursday, Mahmood asked for reassurance that plans were in place for police to move quickly and effectively against far-right groups seeking to intimidate communities.

The protests brought Dover to a standstill on Saturday, with masked activists blocking roads. On Sunday in Portsmouth, about 200 people tried to block migrants being driven away for processing after the RNLI had brought ashore 120 migrants.

## Government response

The Home Office described the protests as causing "unacceptable disorder." Mahmood said: "These scenes have caused disruption for people and, given that people are dressed in black wearing balaclavas, there is a fear factor and obvious malign intent being communicated to our residents. This makes it different from other protests, so we need to make sure we are learning lessons."

## Police powers being considered

The BBC understands police chiefs are now actively considering using recently-granted powers to completely ban face coverings in specific areas in light of the demonstrations.

Under major changes to protest law in the last three years, officers can now:
- Intervene more quickly to end demonstrations that block critical national infrastructure, such as roads to ports
- Designate an entire area a face mask-free zone for the duration of a protest

## Intelligence gaps

During the meeting, Mahmood told police chiefs to make better use of intelligence ahead of future demonstrations.

The call came after Kent Police admitted it had been made aware of a potential demonstration in Dover but had not "fully assessed" the warning before the town's nationally critical port road was brought to a standstill.

## Wider context

Police chiefs say they have initiated an "enhanced and co-ordinated" approach to gathering intelligence in the days ahead. Ministers believe the Patriot Platform umbrella group aims to intimidate communities. It's unclear whether further demonstrations will be called, though local groups associated with Patriot Platform have called for more in some areas.
