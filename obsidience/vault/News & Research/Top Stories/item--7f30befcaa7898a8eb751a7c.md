**BBC News | Business | 10 September 2026**

Primark has announced it plans to launch a home delivery service in Britain, four years after making its first foray into e-commerce.

## What is happening?

The retailer has not specified a launch date but has purchased a warehouse in Sheffield specifically to enable home shipping.

## Why now?

Associated British Foods (ABF), which owns Primark, stated there is "opportunity for profitable growth" through home delivery, which could help boost trade after the retailer reported subdued sales following recent hot weather.

## The shift

Primark did not sell goods online until 2022, when it launched a click-and-collect service requiring shoppers to travel to pick up purchases. ABF confirms the company "will in the future offer home delivery in Great Britain."

## Analyst perspectives

The decision, after years of resistance (including through the pandemic), indicates Primark is facing pressure from ultra-fast-fashion brands such as Shein, Boohoo, TikTok Shop, and Vinted. In July, the chain said it was lowering prices on hundreds of clothing items.

Retail analyst Julie Palmer told BBC News: "Primark has been playing catch up for years" and the move is "as much about defence as growth." She noted the "real test" will be making deliveries profitable at low prices, and that a "slick and effective returns policy" will be key.

ABF predicts Primark's UK sales will grow by about 1% in the fourth quarter, but like-for-like sales in the UK are expected to be "broadly flat" and fall 3% globally.

## Broader retail context

Retail analyst Natalie Berg said Primark is "more than fashionably late to the e-commerce party" and will need to absorb additional costs of returns, which tend to be higher than in-store purchases.

"Not even the pandemic could convince them to launch online, so the fact that Primark is making such a U-turn tells us just how much the retail landscape has evolved," Berg said.

Dan Coatsworth, head of markets at AJ Bell, noted that delivery firms such as InPost and Relay have made it "a lot cheaper in the past few years to send items by post" compared to when Royal Mail dominated.

Other UK retailers are also grappling with higher costs for labour and energy, with John Lewis reporting half-year losses widening to £124m in July.
