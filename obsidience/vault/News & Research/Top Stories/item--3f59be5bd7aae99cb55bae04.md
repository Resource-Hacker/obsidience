## Trump promises $5,000 dividend to every adult American if Republicans win midterms

**BBC News** — 10 September 2026  [Activating Source: source://61ec9ee6-74cd-44a0-9ad8-db93d5ac39ca](source://61ec9ee6-74cd-44a0-9ad8-db93d5ac39ca) | Reporting: [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c62kzr1jg0jo) | Reporting Source: [source://6a4af6e0-f26a-4318-90be-0e32efd68655](source://6a4af6e0-f26a-4318-90be-0e32efd68655)

### The pledge
US President Donald Trump announced at the first-ever mid-term Republican convention in Dallas, Texas, that if Republicans win both the House of Representatives and the Senate in November's midterm elections, he would issue a "$5,000 dividend" to every adult citizen of the United States.

He specified: "We don't want you going to Canada to spend the money. We don't want you going to China, to Germany. You got to spend the money in the United States of America."

### Cost and funding
With nearly 270 million American adults, the payout would cost approximately **$1.3 trillion**.  
Vice-President JD Vance linked the plan to revenue from Trump's tariffs on imported goods, arguing it is "a dividend for American workers" funded by money raised by "standing up to both foreign companies but also foreign countries who've been taking advantage of America's workers."  
Trump had told supporters on 3 September that oil prices would not fall until the war with Iran (started in February 2026) ends after the midterms.

### Feasibility and context
- It would be the first time a US president has offered such a payout to citizens.
- The idea drew "raucous applause" at the convention.
- Democrats called it an "empty promise."
- A former Republican strategist told the BBC it was "a novel solution to an interesting problem" — that the president's party historically loses seats in midterms — but noted he "is not sure where that money is going to come from."
- Trump has previously promised $2,000 cheques funded by tariff revenues (February 2025), which never materialised after the Supreme Court ruled most of his tariffs unlawful.

### Legal questions
The article raises two main legal questions:

1. **Congressional approval** — Congress authorises US spending. Trump did not clarify whether he plans to seek such approval for the "Trump Dividends."

2. **Campaign finance** — Federal law prohibits payments intended to influence voting. However, an election law expert told the *New York Times* the promise would likely be protected by the **First Amendment** as a pledge to all adult citizens rather than a targeted payment to influence voting. A New Mexico lawyer similarly said it constituted a promise made to all citizens during a campaign rather than a payment to influence how individuals vote.

### Uncertainties
- Trump did not say whether he planned to seek Congressional approval.
- It is not clear how money spent outside the US would be monitored.
- A former Republican pollster noted it is "campaign season" (election less than two months away) and described the promise as extraordinary, given the funding question.
