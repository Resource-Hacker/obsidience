**Published:** 10 September 2026, 14:13 GMT — BBC News

Under government plans, local mayors in England will be given the power to impose a new uncapped "tourist tax" on visitors' overnight stays — a percentage of the cost of hotels, B&Bs and other accommodation rather than a flat fee.

## What the government says

Local Government Secretary Angela Rayner said the measure would give mayors the choice to "raise and reinvest funding where it's needed most," supporting local services, public spaces and attractions.

## Hospitality leaders' warnings

UKHospitality chief executive Allen Simpson warned the levy would put jobs at risk and push prices up for families, estimating it could add **£100–£120** to the average cost of a family holiday in England. He told BBC Radio 4's Today programme: "If you only devolve one tax raising power, of course local mayors are going to pull that lever until it snaps."

Conservative shadow housing secretary David Simmonds added that VAT is already 20% on hotels — higher than in other countries — and any tourism tax would be VAT-able too, calling it a "Labour double whammy."

## Political divide

Labour mayors in Liverpool City Region, West Yorkshire, London, the West of England, South Yorkshire, the West Midlands and York and North Yorkshire have welcomed the announcement, though some (including London Mayor Lord Khan) have signalled they would cap levies at around 5%.

Reform UK and Conservatives are likely to oppose the levy. Mayor of Tees Valley Ben Houchen has already ruled it out. Two other Conservative mayors — in Greater Lincolnshire and Hull and East Yorkshire — may also oppose it.

## Existing schemes and wider context

Two English cities already run voluntary schemes: Manchester charges £1 per room per night, and Liverpool charges £2 nightly. Both are business-led.

The plan mirrors schemes in Scotland (a capped 5% levy in Edinburgh) and Wales (a capped £1.30 per person per night from April 2027), as well as models in New York, Amsterdam and Rome.

## Next steps

Local leaders will need to clarify by early 2028 how raised revenue will be reinvested. They can offer exemptions (e.g. for campsites) but not exempt whole localities within their regions to avoid visitor confusion.

The government said it would introduce a bill "in due course".
