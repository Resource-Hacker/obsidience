## Summary

England's mayors are set to gain the power to impose a tourist tax on visitors staying in the country. Hospitality leaders have warned that an **uncapped levy** could risk jobs and push holiday prices higher for families.  

The move represents a shift in local governance authority over tourism revenue, with the specific rate and application method left to individual mayors to determine. Critics argue that without a cap, the tax could disproportionately impact budget travelers and smaller accommodations, potentially leading to reduced employment in the hospitality sector.  

The decision reflects ongoing debates over funding for local services while balancing the economic importance of the tourism industry to many English regions.
