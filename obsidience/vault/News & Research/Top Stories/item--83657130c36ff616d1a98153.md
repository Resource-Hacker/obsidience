## Summary

The RNLI is reviewing its security procedures to ensure the safety of its volunteers after online protests targeting crew members following an intervention in a Channel crossing incident.

## Background

On Sunday, the RNLI helped carry around 120 migrants intercepted off the Isle of Wight to Eastney in Langstone Harbour, where roads were blocked by protesters. Following the rescue, some crew members had their photos and names shared on social media in posts accusing them of **treason**.

RNLI chief executive Peter Sparkes described the abuse as "wholly unacceptable in a civilised society."

## Security Measures

- Volunteers have been told to consider not wearing RNLI-branded clothing in some circumstances when representing the charity.
- Volunteers are "empowered to make decisions to keep yourself, your team or crew safe" and may "go home or close for the day if you need to."
- RNLI members are encouraged to visibly wear ID while on site and to challenge anyone not wearing ID who appears suspicious.

## Responses

- **Sussex Police** are investigating whether any criminal offences were committed.
- **Home Office:** "Directing abuse at people who risk their lives in the service of others is appalling." Platforms should enforce terms of service and "take swift action against discriminatory content."
- **Kemi Badenoch** (Conservative leader): "My concern is about people calling them traitors and accusing them of treason, that is quite wrong. It is the government's job to stop the boats. They should not be attacking volunteers."
- **Sir Robin Knox-Johnston** (RNLI vice-president): "The people who are abusing the volunteers — I wonder how abusive they'd be if they suddenly got a stitch when they're out swimming? They'd be screaming for the RNLI to rescue them and the RNLI would do it."

## RNLI Position

In a message to volunteers, Sparkes said participation in the Sunday rescue was requested by HM Coastguard. He noted that when called upon, the RNLI rescues "men, women and children in distress" and then passes responsibility to the ambulance service, HM Coastguard, police or Border Force.

Figures from the RNLI show only a tiny fraction of lifeboat launches involve assisting people "in distress in the Channel," with the majority of small boats intercepted by Border Force.
