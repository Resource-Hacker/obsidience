Tung Chee-hwa, who became Hong Kong's first leader after the 1997 handover from Britain to China, has died at 89. His tenure was marked by the Asian Financial Crisis, two major epidemics, and the 2003 mass protests against a proposed national security law, which his administration ultimately abandoned. He resigned in 2005. Beijing had reportedly handpicked him as a loyalist before the handover, and his office described him as a man devoted to his country and its people.
