The foreign secretary disagrees that sanctions on Israeli settlements could put British Jews at greater risk.

**Published:** 9 September 2026, 20:59 UTC

**Activating Source:** source://f8df0a6a-09a5-4ea1-9bd0-4f4c7785cdbc

**Additional reporting Source:** source://a7262126-7aa8-44f3-9562-055538018d12

**Key facts:**

*   Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband rejected UK Chief Rabbi Sir Ephraim Mirvis's warning that British Jews are at greater risk after the UK announced sanctions on Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank. Miliband said: "anyone who holds British Jews - or indeed Jews around the world - responsible for the actions of the Israeli government, is engaging in antisemitism, pure and simple."

*   The sanctions on trade with illegal settlements come against a backdrop of rising antisemitic incidents across the UK. The Community Security Trust recorded 1,926 incidents of anti-Jewish hate in the first six months of 2026, a 21% increase on the same period in 2025.

*   Prime Minister Andy Burnham defended the decision, telling MPs that "Britain has to stand for fairness against injustice. Where people are being bullied out of their homes... we will always stand with the underdog."

*   The Palestinian presidency welcomed the decision, calling it "an important step towards confronting Israel's colonial system and its violations against the Palestinian people."

*   Reaction within the UK Jewish community was mixed. The Jewish Leadership Council warned the decision "risks becoming a de facto boycott of Israel" and accused Miliband of "yielding to pressure from the most extreme elements of British society". The Board of Deputies of British Jews said the announcement before Rosh Hashanah brings "concerns for communal security at their highest levels".

*   The Movement for Progressive Judaism said "while the vulnerability of British Jews must not be ignored", measures directed at Israeli settlements "are not the same as an attack on Jews."

*   The sanctions have caused a diplomatic row with Israel, which closed the British consulate in East Jerusalem and banned 11 MPs and a campaigner from the country. Israel's Diaspora Minister recommended that several other British-linked organisations (Oxfam, Save the Children, Norwegian Refugee Council, Islamic Relief Worldwide, Medical Aid for Palestinians) should be refused entry.

*   The sanctions will target companies and people providing services like construction and financing of settlements. There will also be a ban on advertising illegal settlements in the UK. Miliband said the measures could take up to nine months to put in place.
