BBC News • 09 Sep 2026

John Ternus has entered his second week as Apple's chief executive, with the company revealing the first major design change to the iPhone in nearly 20 years. The new foldable iPhone, dubbed the "Duo," features a book-style format that looks like a small iPad when unfolded and a wider iPhone when folded.

Pricing starts at $2,000 (UK: £1,999) up to $3,200, making it the most expensive iPhone to date. Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook and previously led Apple's hardware division, described the device as "inspired by the iPad" and said its folding features are "fluid and completely intuitive," aiming to avoid the "two phones stuck together" feel of competing foldables.

The Duo announcement comes alongside the iPhone 18 and 18 Pro, priced at $1,200 and $1,300 respectively. Ternus noted the foldable iPhone had been in development for several years, and the design shift may address criticism that Apple has lacked major product hits since the 2007 original iPhone.

Analysts offer mixed reactions. Ben Wood of FDM suggested Ternus's promotion was timed to coincide with the launch, while Dipanjan Chatterjee of Forrester warned the foldable "risks becoming a premium-priced ornament." Market data suggests foldables currently comprise only 2% of smartphone sales, with FDM projecting 4% market share in the next decade even with Apple's entry.

Ternus also announced a relaunch of Siri AI, now able to access messages and emails to answer queries and take action. He described it as an "intelligent personal hub" using private data, with features often running on-device. Ternus stated: "Others see that data as something to collect and store. Here, not even Apple can access what's yours."
