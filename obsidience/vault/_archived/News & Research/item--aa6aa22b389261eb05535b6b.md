Google has announced a record €13bn investment in Finland, its largest single investment in Europe, to fund AI infrastructure and clean energy projects.

**Core terms of the deal:**
- A 22-year contract with Finnish utility Fortum to purchase up to 50% of electricity from the Loviisa nuclear power plant, which generates about 10% of Finland's electricity. ^source://e6efb257-3b2a-4916-9f0c-cf5545bd9149^
- Funding for three new data centres in Kajaani, Muhos and Vaala, plus expansion of its existing facility in Hamina. ^source://e6efb257-3b2a-4916-9f0c-cf5545bd9149^
- The investment package also covers clean energy projects and dedicated funds for local biodiversity, education, research and workforce development. ^source://e6efb257-3b2a-4916-9f0c-cf5545bd9149^

**Economic impact:** Google estimates the project will create more than 37,000 jobs during construction and boost Finland's GDP by €3.6bn annually. ^source://e6efb257-3b2a-4916-9f0c-cf5545bd9149^

**Timeline:** Construction is set to take place in 2027 and 2028. ^source://e6efb257-3b2a-4916-9f0c-cf5545bd9149^

**Context:** The announcement comes as tech companies race to secure power supplies for AI infrastructure. Earlier in the week, TikTok announced a separate $1bn investment in Finland for a data centre in Kouvola. Google's parent company Alphabet had just raised its global AI spending plans to as much as $205bn. Finlands Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the deal demonstrates Finland's leadership in responsibly building AI infrastructure. The Loviisa agreement provides long-term financial certainty for the nuclear station and supports Fortum's programme to extend the plant's life and increase capacity. ^source://e6efb257-3b2a-4916-9f0c-cf5545bd9149^
