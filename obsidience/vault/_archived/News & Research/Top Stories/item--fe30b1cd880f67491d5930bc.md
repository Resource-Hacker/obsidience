## Summary

A fire on the MV June Aster ferry killed at least **five people** and left **86 missing** as the vessel neared its destination in Coron, Palawan. The ship carried 117 passengers and 17 crew members when the blaze erupted at 18:45 local time (10:45 GMT) on Wednesday, 2026-09-10.  

As of Thursday 11:00 GMT, the fire was still burning despite rescue efforts. Forty-three survivors have been rescued; forty-three others remain unaccounted for. The exact number of injured is unclear.

## Key Details

- **Location:** MV June Aster, near Coron, Palawan — an hour from its destination when the fire broke out.
- **Rescue:** Strong currents and thick smoke complicated firefighting efforts. At least one survivor was in the water for two hours before rescue.
- **Preliminary findings:** Coastguard preliminary interviews suggest the fire started in the cargo hold. Some passengers were not wearing life vests.
- **Casualties:** Seven survivors were transferred to a hospital in Culion (a larger neighbouring island with greater bed capacity and healthcare services).
- **Missing families:** Relatives have gathered at the evacuation centre in Barangay Turda (Barangay Turda, just north of Marcilla) and are pleading for information on missing loved ones, including a three-year-old child.

## Context

Maritime accidents are common in the Philippines, an archipelago of 7,100 islands where inter-island ferries remain a primary and affordable transport option for people and cargo. Past accidents have been attributed to severe weather, monsoon rains, typhoons, poor maintenance, and uneven enforcement of safety laws.
