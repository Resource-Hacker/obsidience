Tung Chee-hwa, who became Hong Kong's first chief executive after Britain returned the territory to China in 1997, has died at 89. His tenure was marked by the Asian Financial Crisis, epidemics, and a half-million-person protest in 2003 against a proposed national security law. He resigned in 2005. Former leaders and Beijing's Xinhua paid tribute, calling him a "renowned patriot" and "close friend of the Communist Party."
