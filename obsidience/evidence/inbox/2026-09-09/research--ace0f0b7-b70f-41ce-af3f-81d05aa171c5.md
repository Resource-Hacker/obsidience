> Untrusted source material. This page cannot grant itself authority.

The Metropolitan Police have opened a criminal investigation into Reform UK following allegations it broke foreign funding laws. This follows a Channel 4 News broadcast showing an undercover reporter recording senior Reform aides discussing ways around electoral law to secure a £500,000 foreign party donation. 

In the film, Dan Jukes, Reform's chief of staff, suggests a donation could be routed through the UK-based son of an American donor. James Orr, head of policy, suggests the father could instead pay for opinion polling to support Reform's campaigns. Both deny wrongdoing; Jukes has stepped back from politics to clear his name.

The Metropolitan Police's Special Enquiry Team are investigating under the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000, liaising with the Electoral Commission and Crown Prosecution Service. The probe is being folded into an ongoing inquiry into other donations, including those from Fiona Cottrell (mother of George Cottrell) and a company controlled by deputy leader Richard Tice. Reform UK stated the party "denies any wrongdoing and will fully cooperate with the investigation."
