# Raw Evidence

The Metropolitan Police launched a criminal investigation into Reform UK over allegations it broke foreign funding laws, following a Channel 4 News broadcast. An undercover reporter recorded two senior Reform aides apparently suggesting ways around electoral law to secure foreign party donations, one estimated at £500,000.

Met Police Special Enquiry Team investigators are liaising with the Electoral Commission and Crown Prosecution Service, examining offences under the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000. The probe covers the undercover footage and other donations, including payments from Fiona Cottrell (mother of George Cottrell) and a company controlled by deputy leader Richard Tice.

Dan Jukes, chief of staff to Nigel Farage, appeared to suggest a donation could be made in the UK-based name of an American donor's son. James Orr, head of policy, suggested the father could pay for opinion polling supporting Reform campaigns. Both have stepped down from the party.

Reform UK "denies any wrongdoing and will fully cooperate with the investigation". Farage stated he "frankly wasn't listening" to the discussion, dismissing the comments as "loose pub talk".
