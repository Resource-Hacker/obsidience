# Police launch criminal investigation into Reform UK over foreign donations

**Source:** BBC News — 9 September 2026
**Citations:** source://a2d726f3-39e9-4164-b347-954cb885a788, source://c9ba39f4-bd29-4ff0-ba87-d53dbb0904b2

## Key Facts

The Metropolitan Police's Special Enquiry Team is conducting a criminal investigation into Reform UK over allegations it broke foreign funding laws, following a broadcast by Channel 4 News.

## The Undercover Broadcast

An undercover reporter recorded two senior Reform aides discussing a way to circumvent electoral law to secure a **£500,000 foreign donation**. In the film:

- **Dan Jukes** (chief of staff to Nigel Farage) suggested the donation could be made in the UK-based son's name of an American donor, despite the money coming from the father. Electoral law requires a donor to be a UK registered voter or a UK-registered company.
- **James Orr** (head of policy) suggested the American father could pay for opinion polling to support Reform's campaigns.

## Fallout

- **Dan Jukes** stepped back from politics to "clear my name" and denied any wrongdoing.
- **James Orr** said he would cooperate with Reform's internal investigation.
- Both lost their positions after the broadcast overshadowed the party's conference in Birmingham.

## Existing Investigation

The criminal probe is being folded into an inquiry already under way into other donations:

- Direct donations from **Fiona Cottrell** (mother of Farage ally George Cottrell)
- A £1m payment to Reform's deputy leader **Richard Tice**'s company from Fiona Cottrell in June 2024

## Positions

- **Met Police:** "All allegations under investigation relate to potential offences under the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000."
- **Reform UK:** "denies any wrongdoing and will fully cooperate with the investigation."
