# Can Trump pay $5,000 to every American adult if Republicans win midterm elections?

**By Alex Boyd** — **2026-09-10** | BBC News

**Summary** — US President Donald Trump offered every adult American $5,000 (≈£3,700) if Republicans win the November midterm elections, though the proposal faces serious questions about funding, legality, and feasibility.

## The offer
Trump announced the pledge at a Republican party convention on 10 September 2026, where it met with raucous applause from supporters. Democrats dismissed it as an "empty promise." Trump stated the money would have to be spent in the US, though monitoring mechanisms remain unclear.

Vice-President JD Vance suggested the payments could be funded by tariff revenue and would not go to wealthy Americans.

## Legal status
US federal law generally prohibits payments intended to influence voting. However, experts are divided:

- **Dr Joan Mahoney** (University of Southampton) sees it as a legal tax-cut equivalent — "Every party does that all the time... perfectly legal."
- **Laurel Rapp** (Chatham House) describes it as a "very different, very transactional agreement" unlike typical campaign pledges.

## How much would it cost?
Nearly 270 million American adults × $5,000 = **~$1.3 trillion**. Trump gave no funding mechanism and did not say he would seek legislative approval.

Erica York (Tax Foundation) called it "one of the worst ways to use $1.3 trillion" given US deficits are projected to grow unsustainably.

## Can tariff revenue fund it?
Vance indicated tariff revenue could cover the cost, but numbers fall short:

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Tariff/excise revenue since early 2025 | ~$475bn |
| Already refunded by Supreme Court decision | >$100bn |

A White House statement told the BBC: "The doomers and naysayers have consistently doubted President Trump. With the continued support of the American people, President Trump will keep delivering real results."

## Political context
The offer comes less than two months before the midterms, with Robert Moran (former Republican strategist) noting it is "campaign season." Chatham House's Rapp says Trump is "resorting to money because polling is not looking good for Republicans" as he enters the final two years of his term.

## Precedents
- Trump previously proposed $2,000 cheques funded by tariffs — no payouts materialised.
- Biden faced accusations of "buying votes" over a $2,000 stimulus pledge for Democrats winning Georgia Senate seats in January 2021.
- Elon Musk offered cash incentives to voters in seven swing states during the 2024 presidential election.
