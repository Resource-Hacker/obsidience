# England's mayors to be given power to introduce tourist tax

**Published:** 10 September 2026, 10:17 UTC  |  **BBC News**  |  **Reporters:** Jack Fenwick, Ewan Somerville, Chris Graham

## Summary

Under plans to be announced by the UK government, mayors in England are to be given the power to introduce an uncapped overnight visitor levy ("tourist tax") as a percentage of accommodation costs.

## Key details

- Local leaders could apply a levy as a **percentage** of hotel, B&B and self-catering costs rather than a flat fee.
- Revenue raised would be up to mayors and local voters to decide how to reinvest.
- The policy was first raised under PM Keir Starmer in November 2025 and is similar to schemes in Scotland and other European destinations.
- The proposal is framed as part of the government's wider devolution agenda under PM Andy Burnham.

## Hospitality sector response

UKHospitality chief executive Allen Simpson warned the proposals would add **£100-£120** on average to the cost of a family holiday in England. He said:

> "Jobs are now at risk" and "holiday parks which can't open in the shoulder seasons."

> "If you only devolve one tax raising power, of course local mayors are going to pull that lever until it snaps."

## Political response

- **David Simmonds** (Conservative shadow housing secretary) argued VAT is already charged at 20% on hotels — higher than other countries — and the tourism tax would add VAT on top of itself.
- **Sadiq Khan**, Mayor of London, told the Standard the policy "needs to happen sooner rather than later".
- **Paul Swaddle**, leader of Westminster City Council, warned there would be "precisely zero benefit to Westminster residents and businesses" if no cash was shared back with councils.
- **John Chappell**, who runs caravan parks in Skegness, said an extra cost would "kill the industry off" and that tourists are "the saviours of our resorts, not the demons."

## Context

- Manchester and Liverpool already run **voluntary** business-led schemes (£1/room/night and £2/night respectively) where hotels pool money to support local tourism.
- **Edinburgh** charges 5% on overnight accommodation, capped at five nights.
- **Wales** will introduce a capped levy of £1.30 per person per night in April 2026.

Local leaders must clarify by March 2028 how they would spend any revenue raised.
