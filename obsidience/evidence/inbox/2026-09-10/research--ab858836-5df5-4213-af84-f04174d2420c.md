# August 2026 ties July 2023 as joint-hottest month on record

According to the EU's Copernicus climate service, August 2026 was the **joint-hottest month ever recorded**, tying with July 2023.

## The record
Temperatures for the month were **1.65°C above pre-industrial levels**, surpassing the 1.5°C threshold agreed by countries to avoid the worst consequences of climate change. While a single month above 1.5°C does not mean it has been permanently crossed, it shows a trend towards breaching that limit.

## Driving factors
The record was driven by a combination of:
- Long-term climate change
- The rising influence of El Niño, a natural climate pattern in which the eastern Pacific turns unusually warm, releasing stored ocean heat into the atmosphere

Dr Samantha Burgess, strategic lead for climate at Copernicus, described August as "a remarkable month in the global climate record," with impacts increasingly felt by communities, economies and ecosystems across the world.

## Europe and the UK
The month capped western Europe's warmest summer, with the UK recording its hottest summer since records began in 1884 — a high mark the Met Office found had been made around **130 times more likely** by human-caused climate change.

Dr Clair Barnes, an extreme weather researcher at Imperial College London, noted the heat across the UK and Europe disrupted schools, strained hospitals, hit food production, fuelled fires and drought, and claimed "tens of thousands of lives".

## Looking ahead
UN climate chief Simon Stiell said: "The evidence is irrefutable: this is the spiralling price of humanity's fossil fuel addiction, and the global climate crisis it is fuelling."

The current El Niño is still strengthening and could become one of the most powerful on record. Its biggest impact on global temperatures is expected to hit over the coming year, meaning the current run of records may have further to go.

Dr Barnes rejected the idea that such summers are now simply the "new normal": until fossil fuels are stopped being burned, records will continue to tumble and extremes will worsen faster than adaptation.

> "Crucially, how much worse we allow it to get remains within our hands."
