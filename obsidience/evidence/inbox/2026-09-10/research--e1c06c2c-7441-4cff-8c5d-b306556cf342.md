# 'My cancer spread while I waited months for surgery': The cost of England's cancer wait times

**BBC News • 2026-09-10**

Cancer care in England has got worse since the government announced plans to speed up waiting times and save more lives, according to BBC analysis.

## The data

- **71.2%** of cancer patients start treatment within the 62-day timeframe — **down from 71.9%**
- The government's National Cancer Plan targets **80%** by March 2027, rising to 85% by March 2029
- Radiotherapy UK analysis shared with the BBC suggests **2,800 extra patients** would need to start treatment every month (a near 15% increase) to hit the March 2027 target
- **54,000 more patients** will need to start treatment within 62 days before 2029 to meet the goal
- For every month a cancer patient waits before starting treatment, their chance of survival falls by **around 10%** on average

## The human cost

Alan, 70, was diagnosed with lung cancer after a nodule was found in June 2025. He was meant to be operated on within weeks but waited nearly five months. By then the cancer had spread to his lymph nodes.

> "I was so frustrated and angry and couldn't understand why they would leave me like that," says Alan.

> "I used to play golf and get out and about. Now I can't do any of the things I love."

## Expert opinion

**Prof Pat Price**, a leading UK clinical academic oncologist, says the government targets "are now simply impossible to hit", citing a "complete loss of momentum" and a "complete lack of a plan or accountability".

**Steve Haines** of Macmillan Cancer Support says visible progress is needed on the promise to set up a National Cancer Board to oversee progress on improving waiting times and treatment.

## System pressures

Trusts reporting the sharpest declines in performance include Buckinghamshire Healthcare, Bolton NHS Foundation Trust, Royal Devon University Healthcare Trust, East Cheshire, and Torbay and South Devon. Pressures include:

- Rising demand
- Workforce shortages
- Diagnostic bottlenecks
- Industrial action and staff sickness
- Reliance on outside diagnostic providers

## Government response

A Department of Health and Social Care spokesperson says the National Cancer Plan "will help more people get diagnosed earlier and begin treatment sooner". They concede "there is more to do, but we are determined to return cancer performance to the standards people rightly expect".
