# England's mayors to be given power to introduce tourist tax

**Source:** BBC News
**Reported:** 10 September 2026 at 11:02:47 GMT

Hospitality leaders warn that an uncapped tourist levy would risk jobs and push prices up for families holidaying in England.

England's mayors are being given the power to introduce a tourist tax.

The proposal is the most significant expansion of council tax since the system was reformed in 2011.

It is intended to raise money to help fund local services such as transport, waste disposal and council tax bills.

The power would be granted by the upcoming **Local Government (Financial Provisions) (England) Bill**, which is expected to be passed before the current parliament is dissolved in December 2026.

The tax is expected to be levied at a flat rate per night at all hotels, guesthouses, hostels and bed-and-breakfasts, with higher bands for longer stays.

> "This is a huge opportunity for councils to generate more local revenue in order to tackle some of their most pressing spending challenges, but it also brings the risk of a backlash if it is not implemented in a way which is seen to be fair," said **Mike Stevens**, chairman of the Local Government Association (LGA).

> "The Government has made it clear that they want to see a flat-rate tax which would be levied per person per night."

The hospitality industry has expressed concern over the proposal, arguing that it would ultimately result in higher costs for families.

> "We are seeing an influx of visitors across England and it is a wonderful thing that tourists want to come," said **Gareth Southgate**, chief executive of **UK Hospitality**.

> "But if you add to that tax on top of tax and you add to that cost of living then you could end up with a situation where you see a reduction in people coming to England in the future."

The proposal is being opposed by the Conservative Party, who argue that it would amount to double taxation, as councils have already increased council tax to offset the loss of funding they face due to the economic crisis.

The **Conservative Party** has already pledged to vote against the bill if it reaches parliament.
