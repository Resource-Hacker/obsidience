## England's Mayors to Be Given Power to Introduce Tourist Tax

**Source:** BBC News | **Reported:** 2026-09-10T09:01:13Z

On Thursday (10 September 2026), the UK government is expected to announce that mayors across England will be given the power to introduce an overnight visitor levy — commonly referred to as a "tourist tax" — on stays at hotels, bed and breakfasts, and other forms of accommodation [source://bf7cf750-7fcb-4590-9759-28983b24f689][source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].

### Proposed Mechanism
Unlike previous voluntary schemes in Manchester and Liverpool, this proposal would grant mayors the authority to impose an *uncapped* levy calculated as a percentage of accommodation costs rather than a flat fee [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2]. Local leaders would decide how to reinvest the revenue raised [source://bf7cf750-7fcb-4590-9759-28983b24f689].

### Industry Pushback
UKHospitality chief executive Allen Simpson warned that the proposals would cost families an additional £100–£120 on average per holiday [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2]. "If you only devolve one tax raising power, of course local mayors are going to pull that lever until it snaps," he said, arguing that hospitality jobs are "now at risk" and that families would "feel the pinch" [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].

### Existing Precedents
- **Manchester:** A £1 per room, per night voluntary scheme led by former Mayor Andy Burnham [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].
- **Liverpool:** A £2 nightly voluntary charge [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].
- **Scotland:** Local authorities may charge up to 5% on overnight stays (capped at five nights in Edinburgh) [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].
- **Wales:** A capped levy of £1.30 per person per night is set to be introduced in April 2027, left to local authorities to roll out [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].

### Devolution Context
Prime Minister Andy Burnham frames the policy as part of his wider devolution agenda [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2]. Mayors would need to clarify by March 2028 how they intend to spend the new revenue [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2]. Mayor of London Sadiq Khan has endorsed the move, while Westminster City Council leader Paul Swaddle warned that residents would gain "precisely zero benefit" unless revenue is shared with councils [source://73e4929e-da57-4f21-84bd-a607229342b2].
