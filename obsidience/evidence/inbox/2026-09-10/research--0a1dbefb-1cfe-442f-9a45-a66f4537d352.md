## Summary: Trump's $5,000 cheque offer – legal, affordable, or empty?

**Date captured:** 2026-09-10
**Source:** BBC News (US & Canada)

US President Donald Trump has offered every adult American $5,000 (£3,700) if Republicans win November's midterm elections. Key questions surround legality, funding, and intent.

### The offer
- Nearly 270 million American adults means a **$1.3 trillion cost**
- VP JD Vance suggested funding could come from **tariff revenue**, aimed at excluding wealthy Americans
- No legislative approval or funding mechanism has been proposed by the White House

### Legal analysis
- Dr Joan Mahoney (University of Southampton): likely **legal** as a campaign pledge akin to tax-cut promises, since payments go to *everyone* regardless of vote
- Laurel Rapp (Chatham House): characterised it as a **transactional agreement**, unlike conventional campaign pledges

### Feasibility
- Tax Foundation's Erica York called it **"one of the worst ways to use $1.3 trillion"** amid rising deficits
- The US has collected ~$475bn in tariff revenue since early 2025, but over $100bn has been refunded after Supreme Court strikes, per Bipartisan Policy Center

### Political context
- Former Republican strategist Robert Moran: campaign season; money used because **"polling is not looking good"**
- Robert Rapp (Chatham House): short-term move to retain Congressional control in final two years of the term

### Precedents
- Trump previously proposed $2,000 tariff-funded cheques — **never materialised**
- Joe Biden's 2021 Georgia Senate stimulus promise drew accusations of vote-buying
- 2024: Elon Musk offered cash incentives to registered voters in seven swing states

*Additional reporting: Bernd Debusmann Jr, White House reporter*
