## 1. Germany's far-right AfD seeks power after Saxony-Anhalt win

Germany's far-right AfD fell three seats short of an outright majority after winning 43.8% in the Saxony-Anhalt state election, according to preliminary results. It is seeking support from other parties or individual MPs to form a government, which would give the party policing and local education powers. No far-right party has run a German state since World War Two, making this a landmark moment for German and European politics.

## 2. Israeli strikes kill nine in southern Lebanon

Israeli air strikes on the southern Lebanese village of Kfar Roummane killed at least nine people, the Lebanese health ministry said; one strike destroyed a residential building and a second hit a vehicle, killing a paramedic. Israel said it was investigating and accused Hezbollah of repeated drone attacks. President Aoun warned the escalation is jeopardising the June US-brokered ceasefire. The death toll is contested; Lebanon's state news agency initially reported eleven.

## 3. Amazon cargo plane overruns Miami runway

An Amazon cargo plane, operated by 21 Air, overran the runway at Miami International Airport on Sunday, killing five people and seriously injuring five others. The Boeing 767 crossed a road beyond the runway and struck several vehicles before bursting into flames. The NTSB is investigating the cause; officials say it is too early to know whether the dead were on the plane or the ground.

## 4. Zelensky expects war to continue into winter

Ukrainian President Zelensky said he expects the war with Russia to continue into winter after talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Kyiv produced no announced breakthrough. The envoys had met Putin in Moscow the day before. Witkoff called the Kyiv talks 'substantive' and said he was 'encouraged,' while Zelensky stressed the need for air-defence and energy support if fighting continues.

## 5. Indonesia volcano eruption leaves 270,000 stranded

About 270,000 passengers are stranded across Indonesia after the eruption of Anak Krakatau volcano grounded more than 2,000 flights and forced Jakarta's Soekarno-Hatta and three other airports to extend closures into Monday. Ash reached up to 15,000 metres; schools moved online and some shipping was disrupted. Indonesia's Geological Agency warned the probability of further eruptions remains high. Authorities set a three-kilometre exclusion zone and confirmed no tsunami warning.

## 6. China pumps $54bn into state banks and insurers

China is injecting 360 billion yuan (about $53.6bn) into eight state-owned banks and insurers, according to state news agency Xinhua, to strengthen the financial system and support its slowing economy. The package targets three large lenders, including the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, and five insurers. Beijing faces trade tensions with the West, the impact of the Iran war on oil, a property slump and an ageing population.

## 7. Seven dead as Delhi building collapses

Rescuers are digging through the rubble a day after a five-storey building in Satya Niketan, south-west Delhi, collapsed, killing at least seven people. It is not clear how many were inside or whether more are trapped; local media reported two to four dozen occupants, but no official figure has been confirmed. Delhi's chief minister attributed the collapse to water accumulation and ongoing basement repair work.

## 8. Egypt sentences TV presenter to death in drugs case

Television presenter Sarah Khalifa and 11 others were sentenced to death by hanging in Egypt after being convicted of drug trafficking, according to state-owned al-Ahram. Khalifa, 39, best known for her crime programme Mission Impossible, has denied the charges and plans to appeal. Nine fellow defendants received life sentences and seven were acquitted. Amnesty International questioned whether the crimes justify the death penalty under international law.

## 9. Iran warns of 'more painful response' to US attacks

Iran's top negotiator, parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, warned of 'a faster, heavier, and more painful response' to US attacks. Washington said it struck Iranian oil tankers after Tehran attacked American warships, and Iranian state media claimed the navy hit a US vessel in the Strait of Hormuz—a claim the US military denied. The 60-day ceasefire has expired. The strait, crucial to global oil, has been effectively closed since the war began.

## 10. Austria's under-14 school headscarf ban takes effect

As the Austrian school year begins, a law banning 'traditional Muslim' head coverings such as hijabs in public and private schools for girls under 14 has gone into force. Integration Minister Claudia Bauer says it protects young girls; critics say it fuels anti-Muslim feeling and may be unconstitutional. The official Islamic Community opposes it and plans legal challenges, noting the Constitutional Court struck down a previous ban in 2020. Repeated violations can lead to fines of up to €800 for guardians.
