## 1. Germany's far-right AfD nears power in Saxony-Anhalt

Germany's far-right AfD is close to governing the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt after a historic win, falling three seats short of an outright majority with 43.8% of the preliminary vote and now seeking partners to form a government. Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the result "seismic"; the CDU's state share nearly halved to 17.2%. No far-right party has ruled a German state since World War Two, so the outcome carries policing and education powers and is being watched across Europe.

## 2. Israeli strikes kill at least nine in southern Lebanon

Israeli air strikes on a southern Lebanese village killed at least nine people, the Lebanese health ministry said, after a strike destroyed a residential building in Kfar Roummane and a second hit a vehicle carrying a paramedic. The Israeli military said it was investigating the reports, while President Joseph Aoun warned the escalation jeopardises the June US-brokered ceasefire meant to end the conflict between Israel and Hezbollah.

## 3. Kyiv air-raid alerts at a record high

Kyiv is sounding more air-raid alerts than at any point since the 2022 invasion began, BBC Verify's analysis of government figures found, as Russia's strikes now fall during the day. Since round-the-clock attacks began in late August, sirens have averaged ten a day, forcing residents into shelters up to thirteen times daily. Russia's faster jet-powered drones, of which Ukraine said it launched 2,800 in August, prove harder to intercept.

## 4. US envoys make first Kyiv visit, but no breakthrough

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held "encouraging" talks with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Kyiv on their first official visit after meeting Vladimir Putin in Moscow, where a declared three-day ceasefire covered the trip. The push produced no breakthrough: Moscow and Kyiv remain divided over key issues, especially territorial concessions, and Zelenskyy said a "winter package" of US and European military aid would be needed.

## 5. China injects $54bn into state banks and insurers

China is pumping 360 billion yuan (about $53.6bn) into eight state-owned banks and insurers, led by the finance ministry, to shore up the financial system and support a slowing economy, state news agency Xinhua said. The package supports major lenders and insurers, including the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China and the Agricultural Bank of China, amid weak demand, a property slump, trade and technology rivalry, and the impact of the Iran war on oil prices.

## 6. Nepal marks day of mourning after flash floods

Nepalis observed a national day of mourning for victims of a flash flood believed triggered by a glacial collapse, marking the final day of the 13-day mourning period as flags flew at half mast. At least 1,287 people are known to have died, and more than 5,000 remain missing, with rescue teams still searching ground, air and hydropower tunnels. Chinese authorities reported 31 dead and 531 unaccounted for on the Tibetan side of the border.

## 7. Indonesia volcano eruption strands 270,000 passengers

Some 270,000 passengers were stranded across Indonesia after the Anak Krakatau volcano grounded more than 2,000 flights and forced airports, including Jakarta's Soekarno-Hatta International, to suspend operations into a second day. The eruption, which began late Friday about 150km from Jakarta, produced lava fountains and sent ash up to 15,000m. The Geological Agency warned the probability of further eruptions "remains high," with a 3km exclusion zone around the crater.

## 10. Building collapse in Delhi kills at least seven

Rescuers dug through rubble a day after a five-storey residential building, Hostel Daze, collapsed in Satya Niketan, south-west Delhi, killing at least seven people. The exact toll and how many remained trapped were unclear; most residents were students. Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta attributed the collapse to water accumulation combined with ongoing basement repair work; the owner was arrested and five senior municipal officials suspended, while the precise cause was still under examination.
