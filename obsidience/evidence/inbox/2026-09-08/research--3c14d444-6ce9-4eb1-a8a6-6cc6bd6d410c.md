## 1. Canada's counter-tariffs on US take effect, trade war deepens

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on nearly C$28bn ($20bn) of American goods came into force on Tuesday, applying rates up to 50% to items from steel to furniture. The move follows the collapse of US-Canada trade talks in late August and escalates the world's largest bilateral trading relationship. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada seeks a "durable" deal but no movement to resume negotiations has been made. US trade representative Jamieson Greer said the "ball is in Canada's court." Around 41,000 Canadian jobs were lost in August amid the dispute.

## 2. Russian drone strikes on Kyiv kill at least two

At least two people were killed and ten injured when Russian drone strikes hit Kyiv early Tuesday, including a five-storey apartment block in the Solomianskyi district. The attack came after both sides had agreed to halt strikes on each other's capitals for three days while US envoys visited Moscow and Kyiv. President Zelensky said he expected the war to continue into winter following talks with Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner that ended without major breakthroughs. ## 3. Five killed in Miami airport plane crash

Five people were killed and five injured when an Amazon cargo Boeing 767-300 overshot the runway at Miami International Airport on Sunday and struck two vehicles on the ground. NTSB chairwoman Jennifer Homendy described the scene as "utter devastation." The plane, arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico, hit a cleaning van before breaking the perimeter fence and striking a Toyota SUV. Flight recorders have been recovered. Investigators are examining flight systems, crew experience, and weather, including a nearby thunderstorm with gusts up to 26 knots. The crash occurred during the Labor Day weekend.

South Korean intelligence estimates North Korea has already transferred $7–14 billion in weapons to Russia, with an estimated 11,000 North Korean troops fighting in Ukraine. The bridge took 495 days to complete after construction began in 2025.

## 5. Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon kill 12

Israeli air strikes on the village of Kfar Roummane in southern Lebanon killed at least 12 people, including two children and four women, according to the Lebanese health ministry. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun said the escalation is jeopardising the June US-brokered ceasefire. At least 27 people have been killed in Israeli attacks across southern Lebanon in three days. Overall, at least 4,362 people have been killed in Lebanon during the conflict.

## 7. Germany's AfD "very close" to governing Saxony-Anhalt

The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) is close to taking power in eastern Saxony-Anhalt after winning 43.8% of the vote in Sunday's state election, falling three seats short of a majority in the 83-seat parliament. Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the result "seismic" and said the CDU needed "profound reforms" after its share nearly halved to 17.2%. No far-right party has controlled a German state since World War Two. The AfD's deputy leader Beatrix von Storch urged opponents to enter talks, warning of "severe chaos" otherwise. The CDU's own defeated premier acknowledged the "mandate to govern" now lies with the AfD.

## 8. Argentina files criminal case against Falklands oil company

Argentina announced it would file criminal charges against oil company Navitas Petroleum for operating in waters off the Falkland Islands, days after President Javier Milei restated Argentina's sovereignty claim. Navitas holds a 65% stake in the Sea Lion project, set to begin production in 2028 from an estimated 1.7 billion barrels. Milei described drilling as a "clear and urgent danger" to Argentine sovereignty and dismissed the 2013 referendum in which 99.8% of islanders voted to remain British. The UK government said its position was "unwavering." Navitas said the remarks were "not expected to have a material effect" on the project.

## 9. Kenya opens registration for undocumented Burundian residents

Kenya has opened a formal registration window for undocumented Burundians and other East Africans following President Ruto's directive targeting foreigners in small-scale businesses. Hundreds of Burundians scrambled to process travel documents after Ruto set Monday as the deadline. The government spokesperson said those undergoing registration would be presumed legally in Kenya. A 90-day grace period was granted for foreign nationals to regularise work permits and business registrations. A senior Kenyan official apologised to Burundians over harassment. Burundi's foreign minister warned that "if the hate speech continues things will certainly change." The UN reports around 16,000 Burundian refugees in Kenya.
